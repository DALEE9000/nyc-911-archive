9/11 Document Archive

A navigator for New York City's September 11 records

In 2026 the city's Law Department published roughly 64,000 scanned documents on the municipal response to the attacks: air-quality lab sheets, DEP building files, the 7 World Trade Center emergency bunker, EPA correspondence. The official portal is a search box. This is the archive map that goes with it: every box and folder, every building with a file, and the school air-testing campaign laid out in time.

The city tagged each DEP and DCAS record with the physical box and folder it was scanned from. Those labels are the only structure the archive has, and the official site hides them. Pick an agency, open a box, click a folder.

    Source

    Everything here is derived from the City of New York's 9/11 Document Portal, published by the Law Department in 2026. Documents are linked, never copied: every title opens the PDF on the city's own server.

    Method

    The portal runs on a Mindbreeze search appliance with an open JSON interface. A script paged through the index agency by agency and saved the metadata the city attached to each PDF: title, agency, production volume, box name, folder name, page count, plus the first two thousand characters of the city's own OCR text. No PDFs were downloaded. The index also contains a Markdown twin of every PDF; those are the OCR layer and are excluded from the counts.

    Building locations come from parsing DEP folder names of the form 68 WARREN STREET Block: 137 Lot: 1 and geocoding the address with NYC GeoSearch. Folders that are not addresses (email binders, lab batches, EPA working-group files) are in the navigator but not on the map.

    The school testing timeline reads the analysis date printed on each count sheet. Sheets whose date did not survive OCR are omitted; the numbers are a floor, not a census.

    Lab readings come from two passes over the 1,911 multi-page DOE reports. Typed results tables were rebuilt from the city's own text layer by sorting words on page coordinates; each value was checked against the report's narrative range, and dropped-decimal OCR slips were corrected and flagged. Handwritten chain-of-custody forms and bulk-asbestos microscopy sheets (628 pages) were transcribed by a vision model page by page, with a legibility grade and a note on every uncertain cell; those rows are marked "handwritten" in the table. Room locations for asbestos air samples were matched from custody forms by school, sample number, pump volume and date. The portal holds several scanned copies of many reports; duplicates were collapsed to one reading.

    Caveats

    Box and folder labels were typed by whoever scanned the box, so spelling varies and some folders are mislabeled. The "Date" field in the city's index is the date of indexing, not of the document, and is ignored here. Production volumes (NYC-WTC0001 through 0010) are the batches in which the Law Department released records, not an archival series.

    Lab readings are machine-extracted and unverified by any lab or agency. Typed values pass through the city's text recognition and a coordinate re-sort; handwritten values pass through a vision model with a legibility grade. Error modes seen in testing: dropped decimal points, 1/7 and 0/8 confusion, row boundaries slipping on dense tables, and years missing from fax headers. Values were checked against each report's narrative range where one exists; metals-panel and TVOC values were not and include obvious misreads. Treat every number as a pointer to the linked page, not as the record itself.
